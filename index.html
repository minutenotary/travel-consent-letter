Children travelling from Canada

Travel consent letter for a child: who signs

Every parent who is not on the trip signs. That is the whole rule, and every family bends it a different way: a parenting order, a grandparent, a school bus to New York, a twelve-year-old flying alone. Find yours below.

Book with Minute Notary

Canada recommends a letter when a child under 19 travels

  • Alone
  • With only one parent, even for part of the trip
  • With grandparents, relatives or friends
  • With a school, sports, music or faith group

Not legally required in Canada, but border officers and airlines may ask for it, and without it they can ask more questions or call the absent parent. A day trip across the United States border counts.

Case by case

Who gives consent, and what else to carry

Married, common law, separated or divorced: the parent who stays home signs either way. The parent who travels is named in the letter as the accompanying adult, not as the one consenting. The most common case has its own walk-through: a child travelling with one parent.

The letter itself

What it usually says

There is no official wording. Canada publishes a template as a fillable PDF and a printable Word file, and says you can adapt it, with as much detail as possible. If the airline, school or destination country has its own form, sign theirs.

Prefer to answer questions than write from scratch? There are consent letter templates for both parents, a parent with sole custody, a child with a relative or group and a child flying alone.

One letter or several

Neither parent travelsBoth sign one letter, or each signs their own
Brothers and sisters travel togetherOne letter can list them all
Children split up for part of the tripA separate letter for each child
  1. The childFull name, date of birth, passport number.
  2. Each parent giving consentFull name, address, and a phone number and email they will answer during the trip.
  3. The accompanying adultFull name, address, contact details and relationship to the child.
  4. The tripDestination, departure and return dates, and the flights if known.
  5. For a child aloneThe adult meeting the child on arrival, with an address and phone number.
  6. Signature and witnessSigned by each consenting parent in front of the witness. Carry the original: officers may refuse a photocopy or a digital version.

The closest border

Crossing into the United States

For many Ontario families the first international trip is a drive to New York State. The consent letter applies there too, and the border agencies on each side add a few rules of their own.

Day trip by car
A consent letter is still recommended, even for a few hours.travel.gc.ca
Children's ID by land or sea
Canadian children under 16 may show a birth certificate or citizenship card, original or copy.CBP
Children's ID by air
A valid passport for everyone, infants included.CBP
Groups under 19 by land or sea
Birth certificates, plus a letter on the group's letterhead listing the children and certifying that each family consented.CBP
Coming home
Arrive in the same vehicle as the child, with the child's ID, any custody papers and the consent letter. The CBSA suggests having the letter notarized to support its authenticity.CBSA

Who witnesses

Any adult may witness. Canada strongly recommends a notary.

What a notary does

  • Checks each signer's government photo ID against the names in the letter
  • Watches each parent sign, so leave it unsigned until then
  • Records the act with a notarial certificate, signature, date and seal

What a notary does not do

  • Decide who has decision-making responsibility, or read your parenting order for you
  • Sign for a parent who is absent, or replace a missing consent
  • Promise that an airline or another country will accept the letter

If the other parent refuses, cannot be found, or you fear the child may not come back to Canada, speak with a family lawyer before anyone signs. The longer guide, with sources: consent letter for travel, a Canadian parent's guide.
